# Integrated learning progression
Approved direction 2026-11-27. AWS-first cloud engineering with AI infrastructure and business judgment. No new completed work.
## Sequence and pacing
1. Finish current mobile-device lessons and retention review. No premature advanced coding.
2. A+ networking: learn each foundational concept, selectively extend with Network+ material, practice locally, then briefly connect to AWS. Defer advanced topics until prerequisites are met.
3. Continue A+ hardware, virtualization, Windows, tickets and PowerShell; introduce Git and small scripts as useful.
4. Build Linux, networking, security/IAM, Bash and Python fundamentals; prepare Security+ first. Revisit network gaps rather than repeat mastered lessons.
5. AWS architecture and operations: console and CLI first, then Python SDK and Terraform after manual understanding; test, monitor, recover and control cost.
6. Containers, CI/CD, infrastructure automation and observability; Kubernetes only after container and networking competence.
7. AI infrastructure: APIs, data, model deployment, retrieval, evaluation, security, reliability and FinOps; independent capstone and design defense.
## Learning loop
Retention, lesson, terminology, selective supplemental material, practical activity, troubleshooting, cloud connection, business tradeoff, teach-back/interview and documentation. Combine steps where appropriate; one new major concept at a time. Use official docs, free courses and learner-provided inaccessible materials. Do not claim to have watched inaccessible videos. No mandatory separate lab per video.
At 25 hours/week when feasible, use roughly 50% concept/practice, 30% labs, 20% review, documentation and professional development; adjust to topic and fatigue. Coding/networking hours are embedded, not additive. Keep weekly consolidation and 30-day readiness review. If prerequisite gaps persist, remediate with a different scenario before advancing. No calendar-only advancement. Preserve 85% knowledge, 90% terminology, independent practical gates and workplace simulations.
Cloud connections initially conceptual (CPU→EC2 sizing, DNS→Route 53, subnet→VPC); deploy only when ready and authorized. Local first; no paid cloud, hardware or course purchase without approval. Install Packet Tracer/Wireshark when networking labs require them, Linux ARM VM when ready, then VS Code/Git/Python, AWS CLI/Terraform and containers in sequence. Verify compatibility and available memory/storage; avoid running unnecessary VMs concurrently.
AI exit evidence: independently design from requirements, implement and explain code/IaC, deploy securely, troubleshoot unfamiliar failures, evaluate quality/latency/cost, validate access boundaries and rollback, defend tradeoffs in interview. Use synthetic data and include non-logistics capstone. No employment or exam guarantees.
